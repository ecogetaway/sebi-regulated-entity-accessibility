# What this method cannot see

The single most important fact about SEBI's digital accessibility regime is not in any of
its deadlines. It is that **compliance is invisible from outside.**

## The design

Every submission a Regulated Entity makes under the circulars goes by **email** — to
`digital_acc@sebi.gov.in`, or to the entity's stock exchange, depository, or BSE Ltd.,
depending on category. Circular 2025/131 specifies the formats; Annexure A of the December
2025 clarification specifies the recipients.

There is no public register of who has complied. No published audit report. No requirement
to publish an accessibility statement, a conformance claim, a VPAT, or a remediation plan.

Of the twenty-four obligations in [`../regulation/obligations.md`](../regulation/obligations.md),
**eleven cannot be observed from outside at all**, and nine more become observable only if
an entity volunteers.

## What follows

**For investors.** An investor with a disability, deciding which broker to open an account
with, has no way to compare. The regulation created an entitlement and no way to shop on it.

**For this project.** We can measure disclosure and we can measure the artifact. We cannot
measure compliance, and must never claim to. Every finding carries that boundary.

**For the research question.** The invisibility is itself the finding. A great deal of
public writing exists explaining what SEBI requires — accessibility vendors got there first,
because the explainer is lead generation. None of it reports what the market actually looks
like, because the regulation gives nobody the means to look. Measurement is the only route,
and it has not been attempted.

## What would change it

Any one of these would make external verification possible, and none is currently required:

- publication of the Annexure B readiness status, in aggregate or per entity
- a published register of which entities have completed audits
- an accessibility statement obligation, as the EU Web Accessibility Directive imposes
- release of SCORES complaint counts under the "Accessibility" category

The last is the cheapest and would be the most informative. The category already exists —
the December 2025 clarification confirms it, and obliges the entity to remediate the
underlying issue before a complaint can be closed. The data is being collected. It is
simply not published.

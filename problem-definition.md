# Problem definition

**SEBI created a digital accessibility entitlement for investors and no way for anyone
outside SEBI to check whether it is being delivered.**

Three things are true at once:

1. **The obligation is real and dated.** Six circulars between May 2025 and July 2026
   require every Regulated Entity to bring investor-facing platforms to WCAG 2.1, audit them
   through certified professionals, include usability testing by persons with disabilities,
   and remediate the findings by 31 October 2026. It is enforced under s.11(1) of the SEBI
   Act, implementing the RPwD Act 2016, following a Supreme Court judgment that held digital
   access intrinsic to Article 21.

2. **Compliance is invisible.** Every submission goes by email to SEBI, an exchange or a
   depository. No public register, no published audit, no accessibility-statement
   obligation. Eleven of the twenty-four obligations cannot be observed from outside at all.

3. **The public record is explanation, not measurement.** Accessibility vendors have
   published extensively on what SEBI requires — the explainer is lead generation for audit
   work. Nothing published reports what investor-facing platforms actually do. The most
   complete guide in the field still carries the superseded July deadline.

So an investor with a disability, choosing a broker, cannot compare. A journalist cannot
report. A researcher cannot assess. The entitlement exists and the accountability surface
does not.

## And the burden is not where the attention is

SEBI regulates several thousand entities. The largest brokers, which this project has
measured, are the least affected: they have engineering teams and budgets. Most Regulated
Entities are small — individual investment advisers, research analysts, small brokers.

**No circular contains any proportionality provision.** A search across all four substantive
circulars for *exempt, proportion, threshold, turnover, net worth* returns nothing. A
one-person research analyst carries the same obligation as the National Stock Exchange:
an IAAP-certified audit including usability testing by persons with disabilities,
remediation, and an annual re-audit.

See [OQ-5](regulation/open-questions.md).

## What this project does about it

Measures the two things that *are* observable — what an entity discloses, and how its
platform behaves — using a rubric published before the scores, a harness whose raw output is
kept so any number can be re-run, and an explicit register of what the method cannot reach.

And uses the October deadline to ask a question worth answering: **does a regulatory
deadline, unaccompanied by any public accountability mechanism, produce observable change?**

## What it deliberately does not do

Determine compliance. That claim depends on material only SEBI holds. Findings are phrased
as barriers observed against criteria mapped to SEBI's requirements — never as
non-compliance.
